Evaluating CT Windowing for conditional MRI Synthesis in Acute Stroke Imaging

Authors:Paul Banahan, Prateek Mathur, Aonghus Lawlor, Peter MacMahon

Published in: Medical Imaging and Computer-Aided Diagnosis, MICAD 2026

Abstract

Computed Tomography (CT) is central to emergency imaging because it is rapid, widely accessible, and provides quantitative tissue attenuation measurements. These properties also make CT an attractive conditioning modality for medical image synthesis. However, CT spans a broad Hounsfield unit (HU) range, while clinically relevant soft-tissue and pathological differences occupy only a limited interval. Windowing is therefore required to emphasise task-relevant attenuation ranges, yet its impact on medical image synthesis remains under-explored. In this work, we investigate how CT window selection affects conditional MRI synthesis in acute stroke imaging across three generative paradigms. We evaluate windows ranging from broad HU coverage to narrow, clinically informed stroke windows using GAN-based Pix2Pix, conditional flow matching, and conditional diffusion, while maintaining fixed lesion conditioning across window configurations. Synthetic images are assessed using pixel-wise, structural, perceptual, and distributional metrics together with downstream infarct segmentation, qualitative assessment, and determinism analysis. Our findings demonstrate that CT windowing produced visibly distinct synthetic images, particularly in anatomical definition and lesion conspicuity. The effect of windowing varied across both generative paradigms and evaluation measures, with Pix2Pix showing the clearest response to clinically informed windows and no single window consistently optimal across all models and metrics. Downstream segmentation further demonstrated window-dependent lesion recoverability, while high segmentation performance could coexist with visible differences in surrounding anatomy. Stochastic models also differed in output consistency, with diffusion showing greater variation than conditional flow matching. These findings show that CT window selection is crucial for reliable medical image synthesis.
