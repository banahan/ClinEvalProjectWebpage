Determinism, Plausibility, and Clinical Trust in CT-to-MRI Translation for Emergency Spine Imaging

Authors: Paul Banahan, Prateek Mathur, Molly Godson Treacy, Edward McDermott, Eimear Kyle, David Caldwell, Aonghus Lawlor, Peter MacMahon

Published in: In Proceedings of the Applications of Medical AI 5th International Workshop, AMAI 2026, MICCAI 2026

Abstract

In suspected Cauda Equina Syndrome (CES), MRI is the reference standard for diagnosis and surgical decision-making, but access may be delayed or contraindicated in emergency care. CT is more readily available, motivating interest in CT-to-MRI translation as a potential clinical support tool. However, in high-risk settings, synthetic images must be evaluated beyond visual similarity alone. They must preserve clinically relevant anatomy and pathology, and remain consistent given identical generation conditions. In this pilot study, we evaluate five representative CT-to-MRI translation paradigms for emergency spine imaging through three axes: plausibility, clinical trust, and determinism. Plausibility is assessed using conventional quantitative metrics and semantic representation-level analysis. Clinical trust is assessed through blinded radiologist evaluation of image quality, anatomy, pathology, and confidence. Determinism is assessed using repeated-generation coefficient-of-variation analysis. Our findings show that favourable quantitative performance does not consistently translate into stronger clinician-derived assessment, and that repeat-generation stability provides a separate reliability indication. These results support multi-dimensional, clinically informed evaluation of generative medical imaging systems before further clinical translation.
